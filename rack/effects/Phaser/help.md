# Phaser

A phaser: notches swept up and down through the sound. The swoosh on a seventies guitar, a
Rhodes that seems to turn, a pad that moves on its own.

## How it makes the sound

The sound goes through a row of stages that each let every frequency through at the level it
arrived at and delay each by a different amount. On its own that is nothing you can hear. Added
back to the original, wherever the row has shifted a frequency by half a turn the two cancel, and
that is a notch. A slow wave moves the stages up and down, so the notches sweep through the sound.

## The controls

- **Rate** is how fast the notches sweep, from once every fifty seconds to ten times a second.
  Slow is a jet going over; past a few a second it stops being a sweep and becomes a warble.
- **Depth** is how far they sweep, up to two octaves either side of the centre. At nought they
  stand still, which is a fixed comb on the sound rather than a movement.
- **Centre** is where the sweep sits. Low works on the body of a sound, high on its air.
- **Feedback** sends what comes out back in. Turned up it sharpens the notches and puts peaks
  between them, which is the vocal, whistling phaser. Below nought it puts the peaks where the
  notches were, which is a different vowel rather than a different amount. It is paid for: the
  peaks come out at the level the sound went in at, so turning it up does not make the track
  louder.
- **Stages** is how many stages the sound goes through. Four make two notches, which is the
  classic pedal. Six and eight are thicker, and twelve make six notches, which starts to sound
  like a flanger.
- **Spread** is how far apart the two sides are in the sweep. At nought both sides move together,
  which is the mono pedal. At a hundred and eighty the notches go up on one side while they come
  down on the other, which is what makes a phaser move across the room.
- **Mix** is how much of what you hear has been through the stages. It starts at a half, because
  that is where the notches are deepest. At one there is only the shifted copy, which has no
  notches in it and sounds almost exactly like what went in.
- **Level** is how loud the whole thing comes back, in decibels. Notches take level out of a
  sound, and this puts it back without touching the fader.

## Working with it

Start with the mix at a half and the depth well up, then find the rate against the tempo: a sweep
that takes a bar or two to go round sits under a part, one that goes round every beat is on top of
it. Add feedback last, a little at a time, until the notches start to sing.

## What ships with it

Six presets: **Swoosh**, the one it opens as, **Ninety**, a plain four stage pedal in mono,
**Jet**, very slow, twelve stages and a lot of feedback, **Warble**, fast and shallow, **Across**,
opposite sides moving against each other, and **Hollow**, negative feedback high up.
