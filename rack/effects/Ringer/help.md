# Ringer

Ring modulation: the signal multiplied by a tone. Not mixed with one, multiplied by one, and that
difference is the whole sound.

## Why it sounds like a machine

Adding a tone to a voice leaves you with the voice and a tone under it. Multiplying them leaves
neither: what comes out is the sum and the difference of every partial in the one with the tone in
the other. None of those are a harmonic series any more, so the pitch of the original is gone and
its rhythm is not. A sound with all of its timing and none of its pitch is what everybody
recognises as a robot.

## The controls

- **Carrier** is the tone it is multiplied by. Above about twenty a second the original's pitch has
  gone. Under that the sidebands are too close to hear apart and the same arithmetic reads as the
  level going up and down, which is a tremolo that passes through nought on its way.
- **Carrier is** picks a tone or a square. A square is that tone and every odd harmonic of it, so
  it gives the same sound several times over at once: harsher, and closer to old hardware.
- **Spread** carries the two sides a few cycles apart. Nought is one carrier and dead centre; a
  little apart puts the machine across the room rather than inside your head.
- **Crush** takes bits away afterwards, down to two. It is after the modulation rather than before
  it, so what gets stepped is the sound you are keeping.
- **Mix** is how much of what you hear has been modulated. Kept back, the words come back through
  the machine.

## Not a vocoder

A vocoder gets to the same place by another road: a bank of filters on your voice driving the same
bank on a second signal you supply. It needs that second signal and a page of controls. This needs
one knob, and it is what everybody actually used before vocoders were cheap.
