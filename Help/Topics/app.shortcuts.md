# Keyboard shortcuts

Every key the application answers.

## System shortcuts

These are what the application does rather than where it goes, so they stay where
they are.

{system keys}

The first four work in whatever window is in front of you, a machine's panel and a
plugin's window included, because a transport that stops working when you open a
knob is one nobody can trust.

The rest are answered by each page for itself, and a page that cannot do one lets
the key carry on rather than swallowing it: there is no undo for the application,
since what the last thing you did was is a question only the page you did it on can
answer. So undo on RECORD fetches back the take you just deleted, and on PADS it
steps back through the pads.

## Page shortcuts

Every page along the top can have a key of your own, set in SETTINGS under
Shortcuts. They ship on nothing at all, because a shortcut nobody asked for is a
keystroke that does something surprising.

{menu keys}

**A page shortcut is `Ctrl+Alt` and a letter, and nothing else.** That is what keeps
one clear of everything above: every other key here is a letter with `Ctrl`, or with
`Ctrl+Shift`, or on its own, so a page key cannot land on top of something that
already works and nobody has to know what is taken before choosing one.

**The letter is underlined on the tab**, which is where you will look for it once you
have set it. A letter that is not in the page's name marks nothing rather than
guessing: `Ctrl+Alt+Q` on MIXER is a perfectly good shortcut and there is nothing in
MIXER to underline.

Click the key beside a page, then press `Ctrl+Alt` and the letter you want. Anything
else is ignored and it goes on waiting. `Escape` leaves it as it was and `Backspace`
takes it off. One key does one job, so putting a letter on a page takes it off
whatever page had it.

A page shortcut does nothing while you are typing in a box. On several keyboard
layouts AltGr arrives as `Ctrl+Alt`, which is how a bracket or a euro sign is typed,
and a name that jumped to another page halfway through would be a fault with nothing
on the screen to explain it.

## The pattern

The keys the pattern answers are in the list at the top of this page, with the
application's own: moving the cursor, marking and pasting a block, the octave the
letters play in, and whether a typed note carries a velocity. They are there rather
than written out again here because they come off the same table the pattern itself
reads, so a key added arrives on that list without anybody being told.

What is not on it is the half of the pattern's keyboard that is not a shortcut at all.
In the note column the letters are a keyboard, two rows of one: `Z` is C of the
octave shown in the bar, `S` and `D` are the black keys above it, and the row from
`Q` is the octave over that. The two rows overlap by five keys where they join, so
the notes under your hand can be reached from either.

The key left of `1` writes a note off, and so does `Caps Lock`; in the note column
the `1` itself does too. Everywhere else in a cell the digits and `A` to `F` type
the number, and in the effect column a letter that is not one of those is the
command.

## Knobs, faders and switches

- `Up` and `Right` take a step up, `Down` and `Left` a step down, and `Shift` with
  any of them takes the larger step.
- `Home` and `End` go to the ends.
- `Space` and `Enter` press a button or move a switch on.
- On a drawn keyboard `Left` and `Right` change the octave.

## Dialogs

- `Enter` is the button the dialog is for and `Escape` is Cancel.
