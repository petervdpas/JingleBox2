# Capturing a take

Where RECORD takes its sound from, and the controls that make a take.

The top card is the recorder itself. What it captures, how loud it comes in, and what it goes
through on its way to the scratchpad are all set here.

## Where it captures from

**Capture from** says where the input is taken from: a device, an output, or on Linux and on
recent Windows a single program that is playing, such as a browser. It is said here and chosen
at the foot of the **IN** strip on the mixer, since that is the strip it is about. Choosing a
program takes it off its own output, so it goes quiet until **Hear it** on the same strip brings
it back through the desk.

## Making a take

- **Record** starts a take and **Stop** ends it. `Ctrl+R` does both, from any window.
- The clock under the buttons counts the take while it runs.
- What you record lands on the **Scratchpad**, the card that appears under this one, where it is
  played and then saved under a name or thrown away. Nothing is on the shelf until it is saved.
- **Import...** brings files from anywhere onto the shelf instead. Anything that is not already a
  16-bit WAV is converted on the way in.

## Input gain and the meter

**Input gain** is the fader down the right, from -24 to +12 dB. Drag it, use the wheel or the
arrow keys, and double click it for 0 dB. The meter beside it shows the peak of what is coming in,
left and right, and **CLIP** lights when the input reaches full scale and stays lit for a moment
so a single hit is not missed. A take that clips has the clip in it, so turn the gain down and
record again rather than fixing it afterwards.

## Recording Effects

The folded strip under the clock is a chain like a track's: our own effects or plugins, in order.
Every take goes through it once it has been stopped, and the capture as it arrived is kept beside
it. How that works, and editing and keeping takes, is in the help for **Recordings** below.
