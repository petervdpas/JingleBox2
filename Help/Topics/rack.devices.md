# The rack

What this installation has to play with: the machines on one tab and the effects on the other.

The rack is the shelf. A **machine** is played: notes go in and sound comes out, and putting one
in a song makes it an **instrument**, which is your name, your settings and its own id. An
**effect** is not played: a whole track's audio goes in and comes back changed, and in a song it
is a box on a track's chain. Both are boxes of the same kind underneath, which is why they are
laid out in one designer and travel as the same sort of zip.

## The two tabs

**Machines** lists the boxes a song can point a track at. **Effects** lists the ones a chain can
be given. Picking a row draws that box's face beside the list, in its own colours, with its own
menu in the corner.

A tab with nothing on it says so and says where boxes are added, which is SETTINGS, System. An
empty list is a list, not a fault.

## The face

What you see is what the box says it looks like: the knobs, the switches, the keyboard, the pads,
whatever its author laid out. Nothing is added to it from here, so a machine with no keyboard
drawn shows none.

On the Machines tab the face is over the box itself, so a knob turned there is that machine's
starting point rather than any song's. On the Effects tab there is no instrument behind the face
at all, and what its knobs stand at is a bench kept nowhere: an effect in use is a slot on some
track's chain, and two of the same effect are two sets of values. It is still worth having, since
that face is where a hardware knob is pointed at an effect.

## The hamburger

Every box that asked for one carries a menu in a corner of its face. What is on it is the host's:

- **Help** opens the page its author wrote about it, in a window of its own. A box with no page
  written has the line greyed rather than missing.
- **The control surfaces** there is a template for on this box, one line each, and picking one
  lays that template down again.
- **Learn a control**, which turns over the same mode `Ctrl+Shift+M` does.

Pointing works here as it does everywhere: rest the pointer on a knob, hold `Ctrl+Shift+M`, and
touch the control on your desk. A link names the box and the control and never the track, so a
knob pointed at a machine here is pointed at it in every song.

## Taking one off and putting it back

A machine can be taken off the rack, and that is not losing it. It stays registered, the picker
under the list offers it back, and losing one for good is unregistering it in SETTINGS, System.

An effect has no such thing. There is nothing of yours in one, since an effect in use is a slot
on a chain, so registered and on the rack are the same fact: what is registered is what is there.

## Presets

A box can ship presets, and the picker on its face is where one is chosen. On a machine a preset
is a whole instrument to start from; on an effect it is where each control stands. Choosing one
moves the face as well as the sound, so what is on the screen is always what you are hearing.
